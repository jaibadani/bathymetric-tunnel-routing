Underwater Tunnel Route Optimization

Slide 1 / 12 (0:20)

Balancing Feasibility, Length, Terrain Risk, and Curvature

A Benchmark Study in the Hudson Canyon Continental Margin (GEBCO_2026)

Research Question: How do seafloor terrain characteristics and mathematical derivation choices change route feasibility, length, terrain risk, and robustness?

Core Thesis: The shortest feasible tunnel is not always the best tunnel.

Formulations: Discrete Graph (Dijkstra) vs. Continuous Cubic Spline (SciPy SLSQP).

Problem Definition & Motivation

Slide 2 / 12 (1:00)

The Engineering Problem

  • Underwater infrastructure (rail & utility tunnels) must navigate extreme bathymetric relief, continental slopes, and submarine canyons.
  • Tunnel boring machines (TBM) and high-speed rail impose strict maximum curvature limits ($\kappa \le \kappa_{\max}$, min radius $\ge 2000\text{ m}$).
  • Direct straight lines encounter treacherous canyons and unfeasible slope gradients.

What This Project Is & Is Not

  • IS: A mathematical optimization benchmark comparing discrete graph optimality against continuous non-linear programming.
  • IS: A controlled sensitivity analysis of feature-derivation choices.
  • IS NOT: A structural or geotechnical borehole study. Depth, slope, roughness, and curvature are mathematical proxies for seafloor suitability.

Easy Formulation: Discrete Shortest Feasible Route

Slide 6 / 12 (1:00)

Formulation & Graph Model

$$\min_{P} L(P) = \sum_{e \in P} \|e\|$$

Feasibility Constraints:

  • $s(v) \le s_{\max} = 0.08\text{ m/m}$ (~4.57°, P90)
  • $50\text{ m} \le d(v) \le 400\text{ m}$

Graph Structure: 8-neighbor grid graph on 12,321 cells. Metric Euclidean edge weights (450 m and 636.4 m).

Solver: Dijkstra algorithm (nonnegative weights).

Results & Fatal Limitation

  • Route Length: $L_E = 47,955.84\text{ m}$ (47.96 km)
  • Cumulative Risk: $S_E = 493.12$ (high risk!)
  • Runtime: 23.24 ms (9,439 nodes expanded)
  • Optimality: Global discrete optimum on this specific graph.
  • Fatal Flaw: Discrete turns reach $45.0^\circ$. Smoothed curvature reaches $\kappa = 0.004260\text{ m}^{-1}$ (radius 235 m), violently violating continuous alignment constraints!

Hard Formulation: Continuous Smooth Spline Alignment

Slide 7 / 12 (1:30)

Spline Parameterization & Objective

$$\min_{\mathbf{p}} J = \alpha L(p) + \beta S(p)$$

$$L(p) = \int_0^1 \|p'(t)\| dt, \quad S(p) = \int_0^1 R(p(t)) \|p'(t)\| dt$$

  • Parameterization: $C^2$ natural cubic spline $p(t) = (x(t), y(t))$ with $K=7$ interior control points (14 variables).
  • Fields: Bicubic RectBivariateSpline on $d, s, R$.
  • Weights: $\alpha = 1.0, \beta = 50,000$.

Constraints & Multi-Start SLSQP

Continuous Constraints ($M=200$ samples):

  • $50\text{ m} \le d(p(t)) \le 400\text{ m}$
  • $s(p(t)) \le 0.08\text{ m/m}$
  • $|\kappa_{\text{route}}(t)| = \frac{|x'y'' - y'x''|}{(x'^2 + y'^2)^{3/2}} \le 0.0005\text{ m}^{-1}$ ($R_{\min} \ge 2000\text{ m}$)

Multi-Start Protocol: Warm start from Dijkstra, straight line, and 8 randomized perturbations.

Optimality: Local continuous optimum on non-convex manifold.

S-Core Sensitivity Analysis

Slide 10 / 12 (1:30)

Robustness Findings

  • Horn Slope Derivation: 3×3 weighting smooths local gradients. Route shifts marginally ($\Delta L = -0.32\%$, Hausdorff $d_H = 184\text{ m}$).
  • Roughness Window ($w=7$): Broadens hazard footprint, nudging the hard route slightly farther north ($\Delta L = +1.18\%$).
  • Pre-smoothing ($\sigma=2$): Removes high-frequency bathymetric noise, reducing cumulative risk by 14.6%.
  • Slope Limit $s_{\max} = \text{P75}$ (0.0055): Restricts allowable corridor; easy route forced into severe detour ($\Delta L = +18.4\%$).
  • Curvature Limit ($\kappa_{\max} = 0.000333$): Enforces $R_{\min}=3000\text{ m}$, expanding route length by $+4.12\%$.

Conclusion & Research Implications

Slide 12 / 12 (0:30)

Core Conclusions

  1. The Shortest Path is Unbuildable: Discrete grid graph shortest paths contain non-physical 45° turns and hug steep canyon hazard zones.
  2. Price of Realism (+25.15%): Smooth curvature and terrain risk penalization require an additional 12.06 km of tunnel alignment, in exchange for a 75.82% reduction in terrain risk and complete compliance with mechanical curvature limits.
  3. Hybrid Paradigm: Using discrete graph search (Dijkstra) to warm-start continuous non-linear programming (SLSQP) resolves non-convex local trap issues.

Limitations & Scope

This is a 2D bathymetric routing model. Depth and slope are mathematical proxies for seafloor suitability, not subterranean geotechnical borehole stability measurements.
